## Coverage of the combat path

The first movement objective is covered by the movement and order tests in `tests/infantry_path_test.cpp`, `tests/tutorial_orders_test.cpp` and `tests/tutorial_vm_test.cpp`. The original fog of war, spotting and infantry combat are covered by `tests/infantry_combat_test.cpp` and `tests/combat_parity_test.cpp`.

`combat_parity` replays the matched-fields encounter and compares the result against the Gold 2.2 oracle fixture:

- 120 ticks × 13 fields, that is 1560 compared values, plus all 21 discrete events, compared field by field.
- The final RNG state is compared, and the end state of the attacker and the target.
- The asset-free fog grid reproduces the real Tutorial I P-FLAGS and `circ64.dat` trace bit for bit, so the test runs in CI without the private Gold installation.
- Any divergence is reported with the first differing tick and field, and the test exits non-zero.

A second, clearly labelled section of the same table pins native behaviour for the two paths no oracle fixture reaches: a two-sided duel with return fire, and a six-soldier squad that pauses to engage a stationary enemy before resuming its march. That section is a native baseline and not an oracle parity claim. It is regenerated with one command, `combat_parity_test --emit`, when a change to combat timing, auto-engage or pathing is intentional; the regenerated rows must then be called out in the board post.

Mutating the simulation rather than the table confirms that the net bites. In a scratch copy of the tree outside the repository, changing the attacker's reload base produced 414 differences and changing the march-pause gating produced 84, and the pause-gating change left the oracle trace and all 21 events untouched. The second case is the reason the regression section exists.

Nineteen later milestones, Phase 5A through Phase 5S, widened the covered combat path. Each is pinned by unit tests in the same style as the parity fixture, and each is described here from the engine source rather than from the milestone posts.

Phase 5A (21 November 2026) added four retail behaviours that the matched encounter does not reach. A unit spec that carries a `dieexplosion` key names the secondary blast that a destroyed vehicle sets off, and the secondary explosions are chained with a depth bound of four (`cookOffDepth_` in `src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`), so a convoy of wrecks cannot recurse without limit. The retail `attackcrew` and `movecrew` keys are parsed from the unit spec (`src/assets/unit_catalog.cpp`) and set the crew strength at which a vehicle still counts as fully crewed; damage to the crew lowers `crewHp`, the number of active crew follows from it, and a crew that has fallen below that threshold multiplies the vehicle reload cooldown by 1.75 (`scaleVehCooldown` in `src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`). Medic infantry restore friendly vehicle crew in steps of 35 crew hit points, Ambulance vehicles in steps of 45, and an air supply restores the crew in full. Terrain mobility follows the retail `walkonground`, `walkonwater` and `walkonshallows` keys, and the infantry path grid keeps the retail forbidden mask `0x00F0u` (`src/sim/infantry_path.cpp`), so naval units cross water that other units cannot. Battle scoring follows the retail `scorevalue` and `scoretype` keys (`src/assets/unit_catalog.cpp`): the mission debrief reports the running battle score, the enemy losses broken down into armor and guns, and the aircraft shot down, and the per-mission high scores persist in `settings.ini` under a `high_scores` key and are shown next to each mission in the mission menus (`missionHighScores` in `src/ui/settings.hpp`).

Phase 5B (21 November 2026) added finite supply logistics and the retail High Ground sight advantage. A unit whose spec type is `crate` is a supply crate (`isSupplyCrate` in `src/assets/unit_catalog.hpp`), and the `stock` key carries the ammunition it holds; a supply truck keeps two finite stocks, `ammo1` and `ammo2`, which it consumes in use and can be refilled from a crate, with `canSupportVehicle` choosing the vehicle that receives the draw. The High Ground advantage is the P-FLAGS bit `0x2000u`, read by `FogGrid::isElevated` (`src/sim/infantry_combat.cpp`) and exposed to the unit layer as `TutorialUnits::isElevatedMapPoint` and `MissionRunner::isElevatedMapPoint`. An elevated unit gains two sight tiles for infantry and vehicle spotting in `refreshSpotting`, and twelve percent of firing range when the target is not elevated, in the infantry and vehicle combat steps. The hover tooltip reads "High Ground (+vision, +range)" (`src/ui/strings.cpp`).

Phase 5C (21 November 2026) added the retail target-priority matrix, infantry suppression and a live morale meter. The `attackpref` key of a unit spec is parsed into a fourteen-entry priority matrix over the retail target classes (`TargetClass` in `src/assets/unit_catalog.hpp`, `attackPreferenceFor` in `src/assets/unit_catalog.cpp`), so a unit chooses its target by the retail class order instead of by distance alone. Machine gun, heavy machine gun and mortar fire applies suppression to the infantry it hits: `applySuppression` in `src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp` adds up to 35 ticks, a suppressed soldier reloads thirty-five percent slower in `applySoldierShotReload`, and the selection panel reports the state as "Status: Suppressed (slower fire — rally with Officer)". A friendly Officer within 160 world pixels, or inside the same garrison building, is the rally aura: `hasNearbyFriendlyOfficer` in `src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp` raises the suppression decay from one tick to three ticks per step. The HUD selection panel carries a five-segment meter row labelled EX, L, M, A and R (`src/ui/hud.hpp`), and M is the morale meter, computed from hit points, experience, suppression and crew impairment (`src/main.cpp`). Wheeled vehicles bog down in shallows: a wheeled, non-naval vehicle on a shallows tile moves at seventy percent of its speed, and never below thirty-five percent of it (`src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`), while a tracked vehicle is unaffected. The Armored Train carries its own tooltip entry (`src/main.cpp`).

Phase 5D (21 November 2026) added the retail aircraft catalog and three vehicle firing rules. The `AVIA/*` plane specs are parsed into a `PlaneSpec` (health, score value, sight, fly speed, bomb damage and count, ground and air damage) by `UnitCatalog::findPlane` and `parsePlaneText` (`src/assets/unit_catalog.cpp`), and wired into `callAirSupport`, the recon sight tiles, bomb-blast infantry suppression, fighter air-to-air damage and the real score value awarded on `PlaneShotDown`. The retail `gunturndelay` key sets a turret traverse speed and an aim-alignment gate that must be satisfied before a catalog-turreted vehicle fires (`turretStep32` in `src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`), which opens rear-flank ambush windows. The retail `canbecrushed` and `davirange` keys let a heavy tank crush enemy light guns and mortars, and `passcanfire` lets passengers of an open-top transport fire from it, with an Open-Top tooltip.

Phase 5E (21 November 2026) added retail ballistics experience, a save-format revision and several selection-type fixes. The `shot*_accuracy` keys give bombardment scatter scaled by the unit's veteran experience, and `shot*_expa` awards per-shot vehicle combat experience. Open-top vehicles gain `bonus_shotrange` reach. The `seltype` values `aagun` and `mobairdef` are recognised by `isAntiAir`, `mobmortar` by `isArtillery`, and `agit` propaganda vehicles carry a morale rally aura, with `gunboat` helpers added alongside. The snapshot format moved to `SS2SNAP3`, persisting `SoldierState::suppressionTicks` and `VehicleState::hasCrew` and `crewHp` losslessly while still reading `SS2SNAP1` and `SS2SNAP2`, and Quick-Load synchronises the vehicle crew-impairment flag (`src/main.cpp`).

Phase 5F (21 November 2026) added exposed tank riders, command auras and resupply costs. The retail `soldonarmor` key marks tank desant riders exposed on the hull; `damageTankDesantRiders` (`src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`) applies splash, machine-gun, sniper, armour-piercing and air-splash hits to them with live kill, eject and passenger-count synchronisation. The `officerradius` and `moraleautoincrease` keys distinguish the General command aura from the Officer aura, and `moraleresist` gives Generals, Officers and Commandos suppression resistance. The `reload1` and `reload2` keys set ammo resupply costs and clamp the finite supply-truck stock. Retail aircraft audio (`idlesound`, `bombsound`, `fallsound`, `shot_grn_sound`) and the `shot*_endburstsound` reload cues were added with tooltips and translations.

Phase 5G (21 November 2026) added repair economics, gun stabilisation, crew capture and leaflet bombers. The retail `repair` spare-parts cost is charged per hit point in `stepSupplyAndRepair` (`src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`, `UnitSpec::repairCost`), so a heavy tank costs far more parts to repair than a light gun. The `gunshotwait` key holds a vehicle's primary gun for a stabilization delay after moving while the coaxial weapon is unaffected. The `canbecrew` key governs qualified-crew capture efficiency and veterancy transfer in `stepCaptures`: a trained tankist, officer or commando fills the crew, an untrained capture leaves a skeleton crew impaired. The retail unit radius gives infantry and guns a physical crush footprint, and `bombid=fsheet` aircraft drop propaganda leaflets that suppress over a wide area without explosion damage.

Phase 5H (21 November 2026) added garrisoned firing rules, prone deployment and multi-round ammunition. The retail `canfirefrominside` key restricts which weapons fire from a garrison: a PIAT is safe indoors, while backblast weapons (bazooka, panzerfaust, mortars, anti-tank rifles) are blocked, enforced in `orderBuildingAttack` and `stepCombat` (`src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`). The `crouchtofire` key makes an anti-tank rifle deploy prone before its first shot, with the exact retail `crouchsight` value. The `shot1_useammo` and `shot2_useammo` keys give multi-round or zero-ammo consumption per shot in `stepVehicleCombat` (a flame tank burns ten fuel units a shot, heavy tanks three, propaganda and scout cars none), and static fortification emplacements were added with a helper and tooltip.

Phase 5I (21 November 2026) added crew vulnerability scaling, experience under fire and under-crewed immobility. The retail `crew_ver` key scales crew damage in `applyVehicleCrewDamage` (`src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`), so an open propaganda car takes far more crew damage than an enclosed half-track. The `expa_loosehp` key awards combat experience while under fire across infantry and vehicle combat. The `soldtomove` and `movecrew` keys make field guns and multi-crew vehicles immobile when under-crewed (`VehiclePose::moveImpaired`): a pak40 halts on crew loss and an 88mm flak or 150mm gun requires a tow. The crouch-to-fire pose is synchronised live to `SoldierPose::crouchedState`, and `movesmoke` adds vehicle exhaust puffs.

Phase 5J (21 November 2026) added vehicle crew shock, air-versus-ground flak audio and building occupant suppression. The retail `moraleresist` key (parsed at `src/assets/unit_catalog.cpp`) gives vehicle crews shock under high-explosive, flame, air and leaflet hits, with heavy tanks resisted more than light vehicles; a shocked crew reloads 1.35 times slower and scatters its ground fire, decaying via `officerAuraDecayAt` (`src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`) when an Officer, General or agit propaganda vehicle is nearby. The retail `shot_air_sound` and `shot_grn_sound` keys distinguish fighter air-to-air audio from ground anti-aircraft flak, carried in `AirSortiePose::aaSoundGroup` and preserved by `MissionRunner::collectAirSorties` (`src/sim/mission_runner.cpp`). Building bombardment suppresses the occupants in `damageBuilding`, and the suppressed state is carried into the evacuation pose. The `idlesnd_idle` key adds a standby engine sound on vehicle selection, and the HUD morale and readiness rows reflect suppression and crew shock.

Phase 5K (21 November 2026) added indirect ballistic fire, crater scaling and open-cab triage. The retail `SHOTS/*` and `EXPLOSIONS/*` tables are resolved by `UnitCatalog::resolveShotInfoLocked` (`src/assets/unit_catalog.cpp`): a `useh` of two or more marks an indirect ballistic arc with a larger splash radius, `vor` sets the crater scale and `sound` sets the impact sound (howitzer explosion, small explosion, ground ricochet) played in place of the generic explosion. The retail `crewcanbehealed` key doubles medic triage for open-cab vehicles, and the `soundtype` key maps voice roles, adding tactical no-ammo, lay-mine, get-mine and repair callouts and nation-specific air-radio acknowledgements, with Indirect Fire and Open Cab tooltips and translations.

Phase 5L (21 November 2026) added building armour, a mission rating and French voice routing. The retail `LAND\DEFDOM` table gives buildings armour and occupant cover: machine-gun and sniper fire are blocked from demolishing masonry, while Fire damage bypasses zero armour cleanly. The retail `MISC\MISC` `StarScore` adds a one-to-ten mission rating row in the Victory debriefing. The retail `MISC\FRENCH` `fsold` and `ftank` keys route French voices (with a fallback), and `d_vet_*` adds German veteran infantry selection and order voices. The retail `AVIA` `flysmoke` and `fallsmoke` keys add damaged-aircraft smoke trails reusing the existing beam smoke.

Phase 5M (21 November 2026) added altitude-scaled anti-aircraft fire, rocket salvos and garrison range bonuses. The retail `AVIA` altitude and `shot_grn_ammo` keys drive heavy high-altitude flak (88mm) versus light 20mm anti-aircraft fire whose range and damage scale with altitude, the il-2 six-rocket ground-attack salvo, and altitude-scaled aircraft shadow offsets. The retail `LAND\DOM` `UnloadHP` and `bonus_ShotRange` keys give garrisoned units a range bonus and critical-structure thresholds. Heavy-versus-light anti-aircraft and garrison tactical tooltips (suppressed, backblast, critical structure) were added with translations.

Phase 5N (21 November 2026) added mine warfare parity, airborne drops and rider protection. The retail `MISC\MINES` table scales anti-tank and anti-personnel blast radius, applies vehicle experience-on-hit and crew shock, kills desant riders, damages adjacent buildings and chain-detonates sympathetic anti-tank mines, with resolved `mine_at` and `mine_claymore` impact audio. The retail `canbepara` key allows airborne gun, mortar and crate drops through `ParatrooperDrop` and the shared `spawnAndRegisterVehicle` helper (`src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`), with a parachute descent canopy effect. The retail `MISC\MISC` `tanksonarmorprotection` gives riders protection, and `LAND\DOM` `bonus_ShotDeadZone` sets a dead zone for garrisoned heavy weapons.

Phase 5O (21 November 2026) added ground-attack hit-type parity, secondary turrets, bailout crews and fearless fire. The retail `AVIA` `shot_grn_id` key splits ground attack by hit type: machine-gun fighter strafes leave no craters and respect masonry armour without hull damage or shock, while explosive il-2 rockets do not, with a tracer-versus-explosion strafe impact effect. The retail `turret_type` values (zenit, atg_zenit, gaub, atg_gaub) give warships and armored trains anti-aircraft and artillery turrets with `shot2` flak engagement and a secondary-turret ammo display. The retail `*guncrewunit` keys add national artillery bailout crews, and `moralenoattack` makes officers and commandos maintain their fire rate under suppression, with tooltips and translations.

Phase 5P (21 November 2026) wired the mission-script count masks end to end and added explosion blast effects. `MissionRunner` now hands the VM the `countByGroupMask` and `countByPlayerMask` queries (the sixth and seventh `MissionQueries` fields), so OP(3) and OP(4) enforce the four-bit unit-state mask in both the constructor tick-0 scan and the `run()` step loop: bit 0 counts units alive with an active crew or riders, bit 1 unmanned vehicles, bit 2 reserves and bit 3 pending OP(40) reinforcements (`src/sim/mission_runner.cpp`, `src/sim/tutorial.cpp`). The retail `EXPLOSIONS` vehicle cook-off sets off building damage and soldier and vehicle blast suppression, with `dead_flamer` and `selfdestruct` tiers, and a shot-down aircraft crashes with a blast (`applyAirSplash` on `PlaneShotDown`). The retail `MISC\INTERFACE` `UnderAttackInterval` throttles the under-attack voice alert.

Phase 5Q (21 November 2026) corrected the reinforcement passenger order and added retail experience and mobility scaling. The `I-SUPPORT` loader now reads crew slots first and riders second (`p < nCrew`, matching `I-UNITS`), correcting the earlier inverted order (`src/assets/mission.cpp`). The vehicle passenger cap was raised from sixteen to forty-four for retail `stock=20` landing craft and `stock=32..44` transports (`passengerCapacity` in `src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`). The retail mission-authored experience percentage is mapped to the simulation range by `TutorialUnits::missionExpaToSim` (0..100% to 0..0x8000, `src/sim/tutorial_units.hpp`) across `I-UNITS`, the Tutorial map units, `I-SUPPORT` and preloaded riders. The retail `movespeed` and `crouchmovespeed` keys (`src/assets/unit_catalog.cpp`) drive infantry step progress through `stepProgressBase` and vehicle veteran speed scaling through `speedPxMax` (`src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`).

Phase 5R (21 November 2026) added spatial ambient sound, weather visibility, grenade ground-attack, open-top dead zones and vehicle scan and crush experience. The retail `I-WOOFERS` table is parsed into ambient emitters (`AmbientWoofer` and `resolveAmbientWooferSound` in `src/assets/mission.hpp` and `.cpp`), played attenuated by camera distance, and the `@`-prefixed ambient group fallback (`@fire_loop`, `@cicades_loop`, `@birds`) was fixed (`src/main.cpp`). The retail `MISC\WEATHER` table scales visibility — rain 60 percent, fog 70 percent, snow 60 percent (`WeatherSpec` in `src/assets/unit_catalog.hpp`) — applied in `refreshSpotting` (`TutorialUnits::weatherVisibilityPct`, `src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp`). In Phases 5T–5V (22 November 2026), Gold disassembly verified that `I-MISXINF` `dword[0]` selects mission weather (`0=Clear, 1=Fog, 2=Rain, 3=Snow`, `n2Menu_dll.dll` `0x10025d66` / `n2Game_Dll.dll` `0x1006ae6c`), `WeatherSpec` clamps only `<= 100`, sight truncates `(r * visPct) / 100` with a floor of `1` and an upper cap of `31` (`0x1f`), and Easy difficulty (`1.3x`) applies to player vehicles (`0x10035360`) and `marchSightBonus` (`0x1001d452`); `MissionRunner` propagates `MissionData::weather` from `I-MISXINF` on startup. The retail `canattackpoint=2` lets submachine-gun infantry grenade-attack ground up to 140 pixels (`orderAttackGroundSoldiers`), the open-top `bonus_shotdeadzone` adds a minimum range to passenger fire (`stepOpenTopPassengerFire`), and the retail `scandelay` gates passive vehicle auto-scan (`scanDelayTicks`) while `expa_crush` awards crush experience.

Phase 5S (21 November 2026) added the retail commander hatch, logistics and mine veterancy, vehicle morale and infantry death cues. The retail `marchenabled` key (`src/assets/unit_catalog.cpp`) enables a commander hatch toggle (`TutorialUnits::toggleMarchMode`, `MissionRunner::toggleMarchMode`, the `X` hotkey in `src/session/session_input.cpp`). In Phase 5V (22 November 2026), Gold disassembly (`0x1001ceb0`, `0x10060ef0`, `0x1001d0b7`) updated unbuttoned sniper commander damage from `rawDamage / 2` to `MISC\MISC` `tanksonarmorprotection` (`100%` `rawDamage` when unbuttoned) and added suppression-induced auto-button-up whenever `suppressionTicks > 0` (`isVehicleUnbuttoned`, `refreshSpotting`). Three intentional native differences remain documented: `marchenabled` vehicles spawn open (`hatchOpen = v.marchEnabled`) rather than buttoned (`0x1001d275`) for player convenience, `scanDelayTicks` avoids per-scan RNG draws (`0x100046a0`) to preserve deterministic `CombatParity` and minimal command replay noise, and the 125-tick post-combat button-up timer is retained alongside suppression button-up. The retail `expa_reload`, `ammoregendelay` and `healdelay` pairs scale supply-truck resupply and ambulance healing with veterancy and award experience (`stepSupplyAndRepair`). The retail `mineammo`, `layminetime` and `getminetime` keys let anti-tank hunters lay and resupply mines and sappers disarm them over a timed window, with `getminetime=0` disarming instantly. ## Mission script operand semantics

The tutorial VM in `src/sim/tutorial.cpp` interprets the retail mission bytecode. Three operand details were settled against the Gold 2.2 executable and the retail mission files, and are recorded here because the scripts and the tests are read against them. The Gold reading is the reference; the native state is stated separately at the end of this section.

**OP(3) and OP(4) take a fourth operand, a 4-bit unit-state mask.** The mask is not a player, an owner or a unit class: it selects which state counters are summed.

- Bit 0 (mask 1): units alive and on the map. Infantry always count; a vehicle counts only when it carries a crew or riders.
- Bit 1 (mask 2): vehicles on the map with no crew and no riders.
- Bit 2 (mask 4): units held off the map in reserve. Gold keeps the reserve as a small set of lists of off-map entries; each entry records the group it belongs to, an entry is enqueued when its unit leaves the map and redeployed when it is brought back, and the per-group counter is fed from the group recorded on the entry, so the group view of the reserve is a projection of those lists rather than a second list of its own.
- Bit 3 (mask 8): reinforcements that are pending and have not spawned. Gold reads this from the reinforcement queue that OP(40) fills and counts the units that have not spawned yet: the queued unit is counted once and each of its passengers is counted once, and the same per-unit count feeds the group counters and the player counters.

The bits are additive, so mask 3 counts "nothing left on the map" and mask 13 adds the reserve and the pending reinforcements to the on-map count. A census of the 101 retail files that carry scripts, 62 campaign `.ssc` and 39 single `.ssm`, found 287 OP(3) and 165 OP(4) instances, with the mask operand taking only the values {1, 2, 3, 4, 9, 11, 13}. No mask above 13 occurs, so bit 4 is never set in retail data, and all 452 rows use literal operands rather than script variables. The retail figures come from a census of the private installation, re-run for this section on 21 November 2026.

**OP(17) is a next-mission signal, not a player or team assignment.** The single operand packs the next campaign mission identifier in the high byte and the sub-index in the low byte. In `maps/campaign/000000.ssc` the operand `0x100` selects mission `001000.ssc` at sub-index 0, and `0x10200` selects the defeat and retry entry. The earlier reading of this opcode as "player 1 joins team 0" contradicted the player and team table in the same file and was wrong.

**The active-crew rule is what the counts stand on.** In `maps/campaign/000000.ssc` group 98 holds exactly four `sturmtiger` vehicles with owner byte 3 and no crew and no riders, and the script's OP(3) asks for four of them. The bit-0 counter is zero for that group until the vehicles are captured and crewed, so the trigger is a capture condition rather than a start-up condition. Counting the group as simply alive made the tutorial declare victory on the first tick.

Native state at the time of writing. The counters `countByGroupMask` and `countByPlayerMask` exist in `src/sim/tutorial_units.cpp` and are exposed by `MissionRunner`, and `TutorialUnits` records a per-vehicle active-crew flag that is set from the retail placement, promoted on capture and preserved across snapshot loads. As of Phase 5P the VM enforces the fourth operand of OP(3) and OP(4): the handlers in `src/sim/tutorial.cpp` pop the mask and call `queries.countByGroupMask(group, mask)` and `queries.countByPlayerMask(player, mask)`, falling back to the plain alive-by-group and alive-by-player counts only when a hook is empty. The mask semantics above are therefore enforced by the interpreter, not merely documented from the Gold behaviour and the retail data.

`TutorialUnits::countByGroupMask` and `TutorialUnits::countByPlayerMask` answer bits 0, 1 and 2. The bit-0 branch counts the infantry of the group and the vehicles of the group that carry a crew, and for the vehicles the retail placement marks as owner 3 that crew flag is set from the presence of riders or crew, which is the rule the retail case depends on. The bit-1 branch is restricted to vehicles, so infantry are never counted as crewless vehicles. The bit-2 branch reads a reserve of off-map entries that `TutorialUnits` holds as a list of records carrying an identifier, an owner, a group, hit points, ammunition, experience and a pool index; `addReserveUnit` adds an entry, `takeReserveUnits` removes and returns the entries of one group, optionally from one pool only, and `countReserveByGroup` and `countReserveByPlayer` are what the bit-2 branch calls. `MissionRunner` forwards all four. The pool index matches the Gold reserve, which is kept in several such lists rather than one. The reserve is not yet fed: nothing outside `tests/tutorial_vm_test.cpp` calls `addReserveUnit`, and the VM's OP(39) handler still discards its eight operands without acting, so in a retail mission run the bit-2 term is structurally present but always zero. The unit test drives the entry and the redeploy path directly, so the counter is covered even though no mission script reaches it. The retail masks 4 and 13 are answered in the sense that the counter exists, not in the sense that a number of reserved units is reported.

`MissionRunner::countByGroupMask` and `MissionRunner::countByPlayerMask` add bit 3, counting the reinforcement waves that have not been issued. The mission loader folds the passengers of each queue entry into the wave it belongs to, takes the rider count and the crew count from the last two bytes of the twelve-byte unit record, and emits the passengers crew first and riders second (the `I-SUPPORT` order corrected to match `I-UNITS` in Phase 5Q), so each passenger is counted under the group recorded on its own entry. The pending count is a count of units: a queued vehicle and each of its passengers are counted individually, the vehicle under its own group and each passenger under the group recorded on its own entry. That is the same per-unit counting the Gold side does, so the native bit-3 term agrees with the Gold term on the group side and on the player side.

OP(17) is read by the VM and turned into a next-mission signal in `src/sim/mission_runner.cpp`, and that signal is consumed in `src/main.cpp`, in the victory debrief, which resolves it to the next campaign mission.

A headless run on 21 November 2026, `--campaign russian:1 --frames 30 --no-movies`, exits 0, loads the mission, reports three air sorties and prints no victory banner. That is the check that the first-tick victory was removed. The same run was repeated on the Phase 4Q through 4Z builds and again on each of the Phase 5A through 5S builds, each milestone reporting exit 0 with no validation error and no leak for `russian:1`. It was run again for this section on 21 November 2026 on the Phase 5R binary with the staged Khronos layer enabled and `--validate`, and gave exit 0, three air sorties, 30 presented frames, no victory banner and no validation error. With the Phase 5P mask wiring the group-98 OP(3) test counts only crewed vehicles, so the four unmanned sturmtigers do not satisfy it at tick 0; the air-sortie count is set by the mission script and is unchanged by the later milestones.

The native state above was re-read on 21 November 2026 after the Phase 4Q work, again after each of the Phase 4R through 4Z releases, again after each of the Phase 5A through 5S releases, and again after the `save-load-complete-state` landing. The mask counters answer bits 0, 1 and 2 on the `TutorialUnits` side and add bit 3 on the `MissionRunner` side, the VM enforces the fourth operand of OP(3) and OP(4) through those counters, `OP(39)` schedules delayed reserve deployments into `reserveUnits_`, and all 17 remaining retail CAOF opcodes plus `OP(43/44/45)` air-route helpers are implemented with `gold_tutorial_vm` verifying all `136` retail `.ssc`/`.ssm`/`.smm` mission files (`2939` triggers, `0` unhandled opcodes, `136` VM evals, `6` smoke sims).
